Auto Insurance in Ontario

Every driver in Ontario needs auto insurance, but no two drivers need the same policy. Dilbag helps you understand what is required, what is optional, and where the right coverage is worth paying for.

What is covered

Ontario auto policies combine mandatory coverage with optional protection. Dilbag explains each part in plain terms so you can choose with confidence.

  • Third-party liability. Protection if you are found legally responsible for injury to someone else or damage to their property in a collision.
  • Accident benefits. Coverage for medical care, rehabilitation, income replacement, and related costs after a collision, regardless of who was at fault.
  • Direct compensation for property damage. Coverage to repair your vehicle when another insured driver is at fault, with your deductible applying.
  • Collision coverage. Coverage to repair or replace your vehicle after a collision with another vehicle or object, even when you are at fault.
  • Comprehensive coverage. Protection against theft, vandalism, fire, falling objects, and certain weather losses that are not collisions.
  • Optional extras. Add-ons that may fit your situation, such as increased accident benefits, loss of use, or coverage for a new vehicle.

What to know

Your premium reflects your driving record, the vehicle, where you live, how you use the car, and the coverage you choose. Questions about auto insurance

Ontario requires third-party liability, accident benefits, direct compensation for property damage, and uninsured automobile coverage. Collision and comprehensive are optional, though a lender or lease may require them.

Options may include adjusting deductibles, reviewing optional coverage, bundling home and auto, and keeping a clean driving record. Dilbag reviews what each change saves and what protection you give up.

Make sure everyone is safe, call emergency services if anyone is hurt, exchange information, and document the scene. Then contact Dilbag promptly so he can guide you through the claim.

Anyone who regularly drives your vehicle should be listed on the policy. Leaving a regular driver off can affect coverage, so tell Dilbag about new drivers, new vehicles, and changes in use.
